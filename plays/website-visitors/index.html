The best-fit people already on your site should hear from a human while the visit is still fresh, and most teams let them leave with nothing but a cookie. Page views are a poor ranking signal, so this play ranks visitors on who they are: how big their own market is, how strong their offer is, how credible their website looks. An opt-in with a work email is worth more than any anonymous visit, so the loop is built on opt-ins first with identification as the second feed.

Load pipeline-outbound-core first. Where a step says "apply core", follow that section of core.

Ask for these before step 1 and save them to ./pipeline-plays.config.json:

  2. The site map with intent weights: pages that mean buying (pricing, services, case studies, plays) and pages that mean reading (blog).
  3. Stack answers from core section 1, plus the newsletter tool, the form tool and any visitor identification tool.
  4. The lead magnets on offer and the unique offers a Tier 1 can receive: a lead list, a play sample, a teardown.

  • Never say you saw them on the site. Name the page, the play or the magnet the way a peer would.
  • An opt-in always outranks a visit. Someone who gave a work email on purpose sits above anyone a tool guessed.
  • Never gate content to force an opt-in. Forced opt-ins produce dead subscribers; the pages stay open and the magnets earn the email.
  • Identification only where lawful, company-level where person-level is not permitted, neither where company-level is not.
  • Only Tier 1 is urgent. Nothing else needs same-day action, and Tier 3 never gets outreach at all.
  • One person at most once per 60 days across first-party plays. A visitor who is also a newsletter reader is one person, not two.

  8. Week one read: 7 meetings, 5 from the pricing page, 2 from the lead list magnet.

# Website Visitors
