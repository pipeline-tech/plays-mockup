Load pipeline-outbound-core first. Where a step says "apply core", follow that section of the core skill.

Inputs

Ask for these before step 1, and save them to ./pipeline-plays.config.json:

  1. Five to ten seed domains: the customers the user would clone, picked by revenue, retention and ease of close, not logo size. Strip https://, www. and trailing slashes.
  2. The ICP in one paragraph: headcount band, geography, the problem you solve, who owns it, and what disqualifies an account.
  3. Stack answers from core section 1. Lookalike tool: Ocean.io first, AI Ark or DiscoLike if the user already has one.
  4. The offer in one sentence and one proof point per sub-vertical where the user has them.

Steps

1Expand from the seeds

ToolsOcean.ioAI Ark (option)DiscoLike (option)
  • Run the lookalike search three times with the same seeds and filters, once at each similarity floor: strict (0.85 and above), standard (0.79), broad (0.70). Keep the three pulls as separate lists with a similarity_band column. Each band is measured on its own in step 8.
  • Filter on the user's headcount band and countries only. No industry filters; the sort happens in step 2 from the website, not vendor labels.
  • Paginate until the pull is exhausted. A band under 50 companies: widen headcount before lowering the floor.
  • Apply core: lists (second source, dedupe by domain, source column, keep the raw pull).

2Sort into sub-verticals

ToolsSerperClaude
  • Fetch each company's homepage and one product page. Write a two-sentence summary: what they sell, to whom.
  • Ask the user to name 5 to 8 sub-verticals. Examples from the proof run, for a proxy and web-data offer: retail analytics, social media analytics, brand protection, ticketing, travel, SEO, web data extraction. If the user cannot name them, cluster the summaries with the model and propose labels for approval.
  • Label every row with exactly one sub-vertical, or other. If other passes 25 percent of a band, the seeds are too mixed; ask the user to split them.
  • Name one adjacent vertical outside the ICP that shares the underlying need (agencies for a data vendor, integrators for a platform). It gets its own list and campaign in step 7.

3Qualify before anything paid

ToolsClaude
  • Score every account 0 to 100 against the written ICP from the website summary, with one line of reasoning. Default pass mark 60. Apply core: lists (qualify before any paid lookup).
  • Expect 50 to 70 percent to pass. Under 40 means the seeds and the ICP paragraph disagree; stop and show the user the top disqualification reasons.
  • Apply core: exclusions.

4Find decision makers with title keywords

ToolsOcean.ioApolloLinkedIn
  • Pick the buyer roles by company size. Apply core: enrichment (decision maker by size).
  • For each role, generate 20 to 30 title keyword variations with the model: canonical, informal, legacy and regional forms ("data engineering", "data platform", "data infrastructure", "VP data", "director of data"). Search people by those keywords, not by seniority filters. Seniority filters miss the messy real titles, and that is where the volume is.
  • Target 3 to 5 contacts per company: the owner of the problem, the person above them, one or two peers who feel the pain. Under 50 people, one or two.
  • Apply core: enrichment (waterfall) and core: verification.

5Build one campaign per sub-vertical

ToolsEmailBisonInstantly (option)HeyReach
  • One campaign per sub-vertical, standard band first. Strict and broad follow as their own campaigns in weeks 2 and 3, so every band reads separately against the same copy. Own inbox set per campaign. Apply core: send rules.
  • Name campaigns {PLAY} {SUBVERTICAL} {BAND} {MONTH} so weekly reporting can group by any of the four.
  • Tier 2 contacts also get a LinkedIn connection request, no note, the same week as email one.

6Write pain-specific copy

ToolsClaude
  • Apply core: copy and send rules.
  • One pain per sub-vertical, in the words that sub-vertical uses. Ticketing gets a line about blocked checkouts during on-sales; brand protection gets marketplaces that geo-fence listings. Same offer, different first sentence, different proof point.
  • Draft 2 angle variants for email 1 per sub-vertical and one threaded nudge for email 2. Show the full set to the user before upload. A sub-vertical with no proof point runs the general angle and is flagged in the report.
  • Variables: {SUBVERTICAL_PAIN}, {PROOF}, {TITLE_SHORT}. No company-name personalization tricks; the lane is the personalization.

7Add the adjacent-vertical probe

ToolsEmailBisonInstantly (option)
  • Launch the adjacent vertical from step 2 as one more campaign at the standard band, same two-step structure, general angle. It is backup volume and a cheap test of a market the user has not considered.
  • If the probe lands under 1 lead per 500 emails over two weeks, read the replies for why, give it a sub-vertical label, and promote it to a full lane in step 5.

8Read lanes weekly, cut and scale

ToolsEmailBisonAttioSlack
  • Every Monday, per campaign: sent, replies, interested, emails per interested lead. Emails per lead decides; reply rate alone misleads.
  • Under 1 lead per 300 emails: scale. Add inboxes, pull the next similarity band, add adjacent titles.
  • Between 1:300 and 1:1,000: hold, rotate copy after 4 weeks.
  • Over 1 lead per 1,000 emails after 1,500 sends: cut the lane. Pause, log the reason, move the inboxes to a winner.
  • Do not judge a lane under 1,000 sends. Low volume reads as noise in both directions.
  • Apply core: CRM and alerts. Apply core: credits and handoff.

Guardrails

  • Never merge the three similarity bands into one list. Each band and each sub-vertical is measured by itself.
  • Never buy contacts for an account that has not passed the website ICP check. People lookups are the expensive step; the model read costs cents.
  • Never write the sub-vertical label into the email as a category ("as a ticketing company"). Write the pain it stands for.
  • Never cut a lane on reply rate. Cut on emails per lead, after the send floor.

Example run

User: "/pipeline-lookalike-sub-verticals, seeds are our 8 best customers, ICP is B2B software 100 to 2,000 people in the US and UK, we sell residential proxies"

  1. Confirmed seeds and ICP paragraph, Ocean.io plus EmailBison plus HeyReach plus Attio.
  2. Three pulls: 410 strict, 1,180 standard, 2,640 broad. 3,560 unique companies after dedupe, each with its band label.
  3. Website summaries sorted into 7 sub-verticals plus other (14 percent). Adjacent vertical: marketing agencies.
  4. 2,210 passed the ICP check at 60, 88 removed as customers, open deals or recent sequences.
  5. 24 title variations per role, 7,340 contacts found, 5,910 verified valid, catch-alls in their own campaigns.
  6. 7 standard-band campaigns plus one agency probe launched across 16 inboxes after copy approval; strict and broad bands queued for weeks 2 and 3.
  7. Week 4 read: web data extraction at 1:240 and brand protection at 1:310 scaled; travel at 1:1,900 cut; agency probe at 1:470 flagged for promotion.
