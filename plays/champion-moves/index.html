The warmest cold email there is goes to someone who already bought from you, used you or vouched for you, on the day they land somewhere new with budget and a problem they know you solve. The trust a stranger would have to earn is already on record in your CRM. This play keeps a roster of those people, watches for the move, and puts the person who worked with them in front of them before the new stack is decided.

Load pipeline-outbound-core first. Where a step says "apply core", follow that section of the core skill.

Inputs

Ask for these before step 1 and save them to ./pipeline-plays.config.json:

  1. The ICP in one paragraph: industries, headcount band, geography, the problem you solve.
  2. Where the relationship history lives: CRM, product user list, event lists, newsletter replies, and who owned each account.
  3. Stack answers from core section 1.
  4. Which results and customer names may be named in writing.

Steps

1Build the champion roster

ToolsAttioHubSpot (option)Salesforce (option)Google SheetsClaude
  • Export every contact at a closed-won account and tag their role in the deal: signer, champion, daily user. The product's user list knows who logged in every day, and those people move too.
  • Add the warm non-customers: churned on good terms, replied interested or booked but never closed, event contacts, newsletter readers who wrote back.
  • Tag every row with relationship strength (bought, used, advocated, met), the owner at the time, and the result they saw where it is on record. No record, no number in the copy.

2Watch for moves

ToolsApolloAI Ark (option)LinkedInSales Navigator
  • Re-enrich current company, title and start date on every roster row on the same day each month. Apollo for most rosters, AI Ark past a few thousand rows.
  • Load the roster into Sales Navigator as saved lead lists so alerts arrive between runs.
  • A changed company is a move and continues here. A changed title at the same company is a promotion: a congratulations note from the owner, then back to the roster, never a sequence.

3Confirm and qualify the landing

ToolsLinkedInOcean.ioApolloClaudeAttio
  • Confirm every move with a second source. One feed gets start dates wrong by months and reads an advisory role as a move.
  • ICP check on the new company: headcount, industry, location, a yes or no with one reason. Apply core: exclusions. The relationship does not make a non-ICP company ICP.
  • If the new company is already a customer or an open deal, this is not a sequence. Write it as an expansion intro and route it to that account's owner, who just gained an internal ally.

4The hole left behind

ToolsSlackAttioHubSpot (option)
  • When the person leaving sits at a current customer, Slack the account owner the same day. The account just lost its internal advocate; the next renewal runs through someone who did not pick you.
  • Log a churn flag with the departed champion's role and date. The owner maps who holds the seat now and books a call within two weeks. A retention step, and it lives here because the signal arrives here first.

5Enrich and verify the new address

ToolsLeadMagicProspeoMillionVerifierBounceBan
  • Find the address at the new company; the old one is dead or forwards to a stranger.
  • New addresses reach the vendors two to four weeks after the start date, so re-run the waterfall weekly on empty rows. LinkedIn carries the personal note meanwhile.
  • Apply core: enrichment. Apply core: verification.

6Tier and route

ToolsSlackAttioPhoneLinkedInGmail
  • Tier 1: they bought, and the new company fits. Slack the original owner with the move, the shared history and a one-line opener. The owner sends a personal note from their own inbox and calls. No campaign for Tier 1, ever.
  • Tier 2: they used it or advocated for it. The person who worked with them writes a personal email; if silent by day 14, a two-step sequence follows from the same inbox.
  • Tier 3: met only. Two-step sequence from the owner's own inbox.
  • Write every row to the CRM with play name, tier, relationship strength, old account, new company, start date and source URL. If a cold play is working the new company, the champion wins and the cold row pauses.

7Write and launch

ToolsClaudeGmailEmailBisonInstantly (option)HeyReach
  • Apply core: copy and send rules, with one change: these emails come from a named person's real inbox, not a play inbox set.
  • Email 1: congratulate on the specific move, recall the shared history in one line (what they used it for and the result, named only if on record and permitted), offer to show how it would look at the new company. Email 2 is a threaded nudge.
  • The committee ask lives in the reply thread, never in the sequence.
  • Draft one variant per tier and show them to the user before anything sends. Load Tier 2 and 3 with {NEW_COMPANY}, {OLD_COMPANY}, {RESULT} and {OWNER_NAME}; stop at day 90 or on a reply.

8Report monthly

ToolsAttioSlack
  • Monthly, after the re-enrichment run: moves caught, ICP landings, expansion intros, notes sent, sequences started, reply rate, meetings, plus churn flags raised at old accounts and how many got a new contact mapped.
  • Apply core: credits and handoff.

Guardrails

  • A personal note may go any time after the move is public; the business ask waits until day 14.
  • No overlap with New Leader in Seat. That play watches the buying seat at target accounts for any newcomer; Champion Moves watches the people who already know you, wherever they land. A person in both is removed from New Leader in Seat; the champion relationship wins.
  • Always from the person who worked with them, never a cold inbox set. If that person has left, the owner who inherited the relationship sends, and the email says so.
  • Never reveal CRM detail they would not expect you to remember. A result they reported on a call is fair; seat count, contract value or a support ticket is not.
  • A champion who lands outside the ICP gets a congratulations note and stays on the roster. The roster is never a cold list.

Example run

  2. Roster: 612 rows. 318 at closed-won accounts (97 signers, 141 champions, 80 daily users), 64 churned well, 146 interested but never closed, 84 event and newsletter contacts. 221 bought, 119 used, 58 advocated, 214 met.
  3. First monthly run: 41 changes, 29 moves, 12 promotions to their own lane. Sales Navigator added 6 moves between runs.

  7. 6 Tier 1: personal notes and calls from the original owners, 4 calls held. 7 Tier 2: 3 replied inside 14 days, 4 moved to the two-step sequence. 4 Tier 3 loaded from the owner's inbox.
  8. Month one: 5 meetings at new logos, 3 expansion intros, 9 churn flags raised.
